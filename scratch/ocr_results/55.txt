Leading the Deep-Tech Revolution
This panel explored India's transition from Digital India toward a
deep-tech-driven
economy, framing
the discussion around
indigenous technologies,
infrastructure required for genuinely
self-reliant innovation
ecosystems. Dr. Abhay Tiwari distinguished deep technology
from conventional startups by its roots in scientific complexity,
experimentation, and long development cycles arguing that
deep-tech leadership demands understanding technologies at
a foundational level well beyond surface functionality, and using
examples from underwater Al detection systems and industrial
applications to show that originality, not imitation, is the only
viable path.
Mr. Nishant Niranjan addressed the challenge of patient
leadership in deep-tech startups, where
simultaneously balance short-term commercial outcomes
against long-term IP development, building products that solve
real-world use cases for India's distinct linguistic, demographic,
and infrastructural diversity rather than remaining limited to
proof-of-concept demonstrations. Ms. Sumita Roy identified
four core startup challenges defining genuine problems,
building structured product roadmaps, selecting appropriate
technology, and
estimating timelines
realistically
examining capital intensity in deep-tech sectors, infrastructure
limitations in hardware innovation, and the transition from research lab to market deployment. The panel agreed that
India needs more investment, patient execution, and strong ecosystems for experimentation.
