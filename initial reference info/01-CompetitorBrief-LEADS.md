# Competitor Research Brief
**LEADS Next Gen Centre** · v1.0 · Prepared for website project

---

## 1. Market Overview

India's leadership-and-soft-skills training space has grown quickly over the last decade, driven by two forces: top B-schools launching dedicated leadership centres (IIM-Ahmedabad, IMT Hyderabad), and government/CSR-backed skill-development missions (NALCO–Utkal University, Wadhwani Foundation, MSME-sponsored MDPs at NITs). Most of these players focus on *either* corporate leadership training *or* technical/vocational skilling — very few position themselves as a dedicated hub for **non-technical skill upliftment** spanning entrepreneurship, business, and leadership in one place, for **all of India**, across audiences as varied as government officials, students, and industry professionals.

LEADS Next Gen Centre's positioning as "India's first leadership and non-technical skill upliftment centre" sits in a real gap: institution-backed credibility (via MSRUAS) combined with an open, national, multi-audience mandate.

---

## 2. Competitor Profiles

### Ashank Desai Centre for Leadership and Organisational Development (IIM Ahmedabad)
- **Positioning:** Elite, research-driven leadership and org-development centre for senior executives.
- **Strengths:** IIM-A brand equity, strong research output, consulting arm.
- **Weaknesses:** High barrier to entry (fees, seniority), not designed for students or early founders.
- **Design/Content notes:** Institutional, text-heavy, minimal visual storytelling; site reads like an academic department page, not a destination brand.

### Centre for Leadership Development (IMT Hyderabad)
- **Positioning:** B-school-hosted centre running invited talks, seminars, and case-writing with industry.
- **Strengths:** Academic rigor, MoUs with industry bodies.
- **Weaknesses:** Event/activity-led rather than platform-led; little sense of an ongoing "movement" or community.
- **Design/Content notes:** Brochure-style presentation; static, low interactivity.

### EDII "Digital Leaders" Programme (EDII Ahmedabad / IIT Madras / IIM Trichy)
- **Positioning:** Executive-education style short programme in tech entrepreneurship and intrapreneurship.
- **Strengths:** Multi-institution faculty credibility, clear program structure.
- **Weaknesses:** Programme-specific microsite, not a standing "centre" brand; narrow tech-entrepreneurship focus, not broad non-technical skilling.
- **Design/Content notes:** Brochure/PDF-first content, not a living website with recurring events.

### Wadhwani Foundation
- **Strengths:** National scale, strong content marketing (blog cadence on entrepreneurship/skills), broad media coverage.
- **Weaknesses:** Foundation-scale, less personal/community feel; not tied to a physical "centre" or flagship summit.
- **Design/Content notes:** Strong blog engine, but the site feels like a media outlet more than a centre with a face and a place.

## 3. Gap Analysis

| Industry Norm | What None of Them Do Well | LEADS Opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Programme/brochure-first sites | Show a living calendar of *ongoing* events with visual identity per event | Dedicated Events experience with distinct branding per event (Catalyst, BLS, etc.) shown as engaging pop-ups |
| Senior-executive-only framing | Speak equally to students, founders, government, and industry in one voice | Single centre serving the full spectrum — "anyone in India who wants to achieve something" |
| Static PDFs/brochures for impact | Make impact reports easy to browse and download | Dedicated Impact Reports section with clear, frictionless downloads |
| Generic "Contact Us" forms | Treat inbound interest as a real funnel to nurture | Purpose-built lead capture tied to a genuine follow-up process |
| One flagship credential (a single institute) | Connect a flagship *summit* brand (like BLS) into the main site | Direct, prominent link out to the Bharath Leadership Summit |

---

## 4. Audience Sentiment

Across founder/professional forums and program reviews, recurring themes: people want **credibility** (institutional backing, real numbers, real speakers) and **relevance to their stage** — students want accessible entry points, professionals want networking and tangible skills, government/industry stakeholders want serious tone and social/national impact framing. A common complaint about existing centres is that they feel like "one more course" rather than an ongoing ecosystem or community.

---

## 5. Design Patterns to Break

- Break from: dense, text-only academic pages with no visual identity.
- Break from: treating every event as a forgettable, one-line news item.
- Keep: a serious, credible visual tone (LEADS' audience includes government and industry) — but paired with more energy and motion than typical B-school centre sites, to also engage students.

---

## 6. Content Opportunities

- "What is non-technical skill upliftment?" — no competitor clearly defines or owns this category. LEADS can.
- Content built around real numbers from Year 1 (30+ events, flagship summit) — most competitors under-use their own achievements.
- FAQ-style content answering "how do I get involved with LEADS" for different visitor types (student / founder / government / industry).

## 7. Strategic Recommendations

1. Own the phrase "non-technical skill upliftment centre" as a category-defining term across the site.
2. Give the Events section real visual identity per event — this is a clear differentiator versus every competitor reviewed.
3. Lead with numbers (events hosted, summit scale) on the homepage — competitors bury their proof points.
4. Make the BLS link feel like a natural extension of LEADS, not an afterthought — visitors should understand it's the centre's flagship summit.
5. Build the Impact Reports section for skimmability — one-click access, not multi-step PDF hunting.
6. Design the Contact/lead form as a genuine funnel entry point, with distinct paths for students vs. professionals vs. institutions.
7. Keep the tone credible enough for government and industry, while using colour, motion, and imagery to stay engaging for students.
